# Plain-English Rewriter

**Dense text in, plain English out. Or flip it: rough notes in, a properly structured document out.**

No industry or role assumptions are built in. It works on anything.

## Two directions

| You paste | You get |
|-----------|---------|
| A policy, contract clause, technical spec, government letter, or anything full of jargon | The same content in plain English, with the meaning kept and any remaining terms explained |
| Dot points, meeting scribbles, a brain-dump, shorthand notes | A structured document with a title, headings and full sentences, with placeholders where details are missing |

The skill works out which direction you need. If it isn't obvious, it will ask.

## Try it

**Simplify:**

> Put this in plain English:
>
> *[paste the dense text]*

**Structure:**

> Turn these notes into a proper document:
>
> *[paste your notes]*

Other ways to ask:

- "What does this clause actually mean for us?"
- "Rewrite this policy so new starters can understand it."
- "Turn my notes into a one-page proposal for the leadership team."
- "Show me the original and the plain-English version side by side."

Nothing to test with? Use [`examples/sample-inputs.md`](examples/sample-inputs.md). It has one example for each direction.

## Tips

- Say who it's for ("for customers", "for the board", "for new staff") and the rewrite will be pitched at them.
- In Structure mode, the skill won't invent facts. It leaves placeholders like **[date]** and lists them at the end so you know what to fill in.
- For legal or regulatory text, the plain-English version helps you understand it. The original wording is still what counts.
